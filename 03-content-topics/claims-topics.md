## Transferable careers into claims

### Series: Occupation → Claims
Core viewer transformation: **“I didn’t realize my experience could do THAT.”**

Instead of starting with a claims job title, start with the viewer’s current occupation and decode what they actually do:
**occupation → tasks → business functions → transferable claims competencies → claims/insurance lanes**

The point is not to say every occupation automatically qualifies someone for every claims role. The point is to reveal hidden overlap and then identify what gaps would still need to be closed.

Possible hook structures:
- “If you work in [occupation], here’s how your experience connects to claims.”
- “You think you have no claims experience, but if you do this at work every day, look at this.”
- “Here’s what a [nurse/teacher/customer service rep/banker/etc.] is already doing that exists inside claims.”
- “Before you start over, let me translate what you already know how to do.”
- “You call it [task]. Insurance calls that [business function].”
- “If this is your job, these are the claims lanes I would look at first.”

Examples to develop: nursing/medical, customer service, USPS/logistics, banking, care coordination, litigation/legal, construction, retail, teaching, trucking, operations.

### Series: Claims Is All Around You / Claims Corner
Show how claims logic and claims functions appear in everyday occupations, industries, situations, and businesses—even when the title does not say “claims.”

Core idea: **Claims is bigger than the title “claims adjuster.”**

Recurring lens:
**What happened? → What is covered/responsible? → What evidence exists? → What is the value/damage? → What should happen next?**

Use ordinary situations and occupations to reveal investigation, documentation, negotiation, evaluation, liability, risk, resolution, compliance, case management, and settlement logic.

Possible hooks:
- “You don’t work in claims, but you’re already using claims logic.”
- “Claims is literally all around you. Look at what this job is actually doing.”
- “This doesn’t say ‘claims adjuster’ anywhere in the title—but watch the functions.”
- “Welcome to Claims Corner: let me show you where the claims work is hiding.”
- “That job you do every day? Here’s the claims version of it.”

### Strategic distinction between the two series
**Occupation → Claims** = viewer-centered career translation. Start with *their job* and show where their existing value could move.

**Claims Is All Around You / Claims Corner** = worldview/education. Start with an everyday job, event, or system and reveal the claims logic hiding inside it.

Both reinforce the larger Victoria belief:
**Your career is bigger than your job title, and the market cannot reward value it cannot see.**

## Claims career myths

### Non-CAT / hidden-lanes belief reversal
Use content to break the mental model that claims = hurricanes + roofs + storm chasing.

Example proven direction:
“You can make strong money in claims without chasing storms and climbing roofs.”

Follow-on angles:
- Claims roles that never require climbing a roof
- Desk vs. field vs. operations vs. specialty claims
- Claims work people mistake for something else
- Six-figure business functions inside the claims ecosystem
- “If CAT is the only claims career you know, your map of the industry is incomplete.”
