# Framework-First Content Engine

## Core Discovery

Victoria's content machine should NOT primarily generate from:
"topic → script"

It should generate from:

**FRAMEWORK / MECHANISM**
→ **AUDIENCE PROBLEM**
→ **CREATIVE WRAPPER**
→ **SCRIPT**

This is the major insight pulled from studying Shelby's content.

The observed Shelby videos do not appear to be one rigid personal sales process.

Instead, they appear to teach a library of:
- sales frameworks
- persuasion principles
- psychology mechanisms
- positioning concepts
- discovery methods
- urgency mechanisms
- identity mechanisms

Different videos select different mechanisms depending on the scenario.

Important:
Do not claim these scripts prove Shelby personally sells through one specific sequence.
They demonstrate concepts she teaches.

If content starts with topics only:

# EXAMPLE

Framework:
**Gap Diagnosis**

Audience:
Experienced adjuster

Problem:
6 years experience but no good offers

Wrappers:

### Straight Reframe
"You don't have an experience problem. You have a gap between what you've done and what employers can see."

### Analogy
"Your resume is like having $100K in the bank but showing the lender an empty account."

### Demonstrate the Brain
"Okay, six years in claims? Tell me what you handled..."

### Comment Response
Use real comment:
"I've been an adjuster for six years and can't find a decent job."

### Truth Stack
"Years in claims don't guarantee leverage. Titles don't equal value. Experience nobody can interpret might as well be invisible..."

Same framework.
Five different videos.

# VICTORIA FRAMEWORK LIBRARY — INITIAL

The Content Machine should develop a growing library of Victoria frameworks.

## 1. Gap Diagnosis

Core question:
What do they want versus what are they currently doing?

Use for:
- job search
- interviews
- salary
- career growth
- mentorship

---

## 2. Transferable Value

Core principle:
People often have relevant experience but do not know how to translate it.

Use for:
- career changers
- medical billing
- operations
- retail
- banking
- government
- customer service

---

## 3. Proof Gap

Core principle:
Being capable and proving capability are different.

Use for:
- resumes
- interviews
- portfolios
- certifications
- experience

---

## 4. Title Trap

Core principle:
Searching only one job title can hide adjacent opportunities.

Use for:
- experienced adjusters
- claims expansion
- specialty roles
- risk/compliance/operations

---

## 5. Positioning

Core principle:
The same experience can be perceived differently depending on how it is framed for the role.

Use for:
- resumes
- interviews
- LinkedIn
- applications
- compensation

---

## 6. Employer Risk Reduction

Core principle:
Employers often choose candidates who feel easier/safer to hire.

Questions:
- Can they do the work?
- Can they explain it?
- Have they handled similar problems?
- How much training/risk is involved?

Use for:
- interviews
- resume strategy
- certifications
- role changes

---

## 7. Optionality / Leverage

Core principle:
More credible options create stronger career leverage.

Use for:
- job titles
- licenses
- specialties
- negotiation
- employers
- career stability

## 11. Market Fit

Core principle:

## 12. Career Ceiling

Core principle:

## 14. Search Strategy

## 15. Identity / Career Self-Concept
