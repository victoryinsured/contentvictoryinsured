# Audience Targeting Principles

## Core principle: Broad entrance → specific destination

A strong video can begin with a problem, desire, or emotional situation that a large audience recognizes, then narrow toward the specific person Victoria actually wants to reach.

### Why this matters
The goal is not generic virality. The goal is qualified virality: broad enough to earn reach, specific enough that the intended viewer thinks, "She is talking to me."

### Structure
1. Broad human tension, desire, or problem
2. Specific situation or audience qualifier
3. Victoria's diagnosis, story, or teaching
4. Niche-specific example or opportunity
5. Actionable takeaway / next step

### Example
Broad entrance:
"If you keep applying to jobs you technically qualify for but nobody is calling you back..."

Specific destination:
"...especially if you're trying to move into claims, risk, fraud, operations, or another role where your experience translates but your resume isn't making the connection obvious."

### Rule
Do not make the opening so broad that TikTok cannot classify the topic or so narrow that nobody outside the exact niche can enter the story.

Use spoken language, on-screen text, caption, and relevant keywords together to make the intended topic obvious.

## One-person delivery
Even when speaking to a large audience, write and deliver the script as though Victoria is talking to one real person with one real problem.

Use:
- "you"
- specific situations
- recognizable frustrations
- direct advice
- natural conversational reactions

Avoid:
- generic motivational language
- vague "everyone" statements
- corporate-sounding audience labels unless useful for classification
