# TikTok 7-Second Production Rules

## Victoria's observed rules

### 1. On-screen text matters
The on-screen text must:
- make sense with the sound
- complete the joke / message / trend mechanic
- communicate the niche quickly
- not sound AI-written
- be readable fast

### 2. Caption must fit
The caption should support the video without sounding generic, over-written, or AI-generated.

### 3. Hashtags should support classification
Use hashtags to help TikTok understand the topic and audience. Do not rely on generic viral tags alone.

### 4. Analyze whether the sound is actually viral
For each high-performing post, determine:
- was the sound trending at the time?
- was it a creator/original sound Victoria simply liked?
- was the trend itself driving discovery?
- or was the content strong enough to perform regardless of sound?

### 5. Grid readability is required
After posting, the video thumbnail/grid preview must clearly show what the video is about.

The cover/on-screen text should be legible from the TikTok profile grid without opening the video.

### 6. Grid text should function like a headline
A visitor landing on Victoria's profile should be able to scan the grid and immediately understand:
- what the video is about
- why it may be relevant to them
- what type of career/claims insight they will get

Avoid vague grid text that only makes sense after watching.

## Visual pattern observed from Victoria's current grid
- strong talking-head presence
- large readable text overlays
- topic-driven covers
- frequent use of "When...", "POV...", "Step by step...", and result-driven statements
- videos communicate subject matter at a glance
- visual variety in location/background while keeping Victoria as the consistent face

## Content Brain requirement
For every 7-second post, generate:
1. sound
2. on-screen text
3. grid-safe headline version
4. caption
5. hashtags
6. trend classification
7. niche classification
8. reason the audio + text fit each other
