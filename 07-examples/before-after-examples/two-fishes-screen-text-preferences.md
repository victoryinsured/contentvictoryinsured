# Two Fishes — Screen Text Preference Training

## Strong / approved direction

### 1
"Me explaining that insurance has way more six-figure jobs than sales, roofs, and storm deployments."

Why it works:
- Fits the sound's "why are we acting like the options are limited?" logic
- Broadens the audience beyond CAT
- Reinforces Victory's blue-ocean positioning
- Feels informational without becoming dry

### 2
"When people think claims only pays well if you're chasing hurricanes."

Why it works:
- Strong misconception setup
- Classy shade toward CAT
- Lets the audio function as the rebuttal
- Clear niche signal

## Partially approved

### 3
"POV: you finally realize the highest-paying part of insurance might be the part nobody ever told you existed."

Victoria likes the core idea but not the full wording.

What to preserve:
- Hidden / overlooked part of insurance
- Discovery / realization
- High-value career opportunity

What to improve:
- Less vague
- More natural Victoria phrasing
- Make the sound complete the thought more clearly

### 4
"Me trying to explain that 'claims adjuster' is not one job… it's an entire career ecosystem."

Victoria likes:
"Me trying to explain that 'claims adjuster' is not one job..."

Victoria dislikes:
"it's an entire career ecosystem"

Reason:
- Feels too formal / consultant-like
- Not how Victoria would naturally say it

Better direction:
"Me trying to explain that 'claims adjuster' isn't just chasing storms."

## Rejected

### 5
"When you stop asking 'how do I become an adjuster?' and start asking 'which part of claims actually fits me?'"

Why rejected:
- Good career strategy thought, but does NOT fit this sound
- It becomes a standalone lesson instead of completing the audio's logic

## Important style rule
A good Victoria screen-text idea can still be rejected if it does not fit the specific sound.

For this sound, preferred text should feel like:
- "Me explaining..."
- "When people think..."
- a misconception / false limitation
- an explanation or rebuttal the sound can help carry

Avoid turning the trend into:
- generic career advice
- a motivational lesson
- a strategic question that could work with any audio
