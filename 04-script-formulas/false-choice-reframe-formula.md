# Formula: False-Choice Reframe

## Purpose
Use when a trending sound or spoken audio carries the logic:
- "Why are we acting like I have to choose?"
- "Both can be true."
- "You're assuming the options are limited."
- "Let me explain why that assumption is wrong."

This is especially useful for sounds where the audio feels like a rebuttal, justification, explanation, or "actually..." response.

## Core formula

LIMITING BELIEF / FALSE CHOICE → VICTORIA REBUTTAL → BETTER POSSIBILITY

Short version:

WHAT PEOPLE THINK → WHAT VICTORIA KNOWS → WHAT'S ACTUALLY POSSIBLE

## Victoria-specific screen text patterns

### Pattern A — "Me explaining..."
"Me explaining that [better outcome] is possible without [assumed tradeoff]."

Example:
"Me explaining that you can make $4,000 a week working insurance claims without chasing storms, climbing roofs, or becoming a salesman."

### Pattern B — "When people think..."
"When people think [common misconception]."

The audio acts as Victoria's rebuttal.

Example:
"When people think claims only pays well if you're chasing hurricanes."

### Pattern C — "Me trying to explain..."
"Me trying to explain that [role / industry] isn't just [stereotype]."

Example:
"Me trying to explain that 'claims adjuster' isn't just chasing storms."

### Pattern D — Hidden possibility
"POV: you realize [better / overlooked possibility]."

Use carefully. Must still match the audio's rebuttal logic.

## Required inputs
Before generating:
1. The sound's literal meaning
2. The sound's TikTok/community meaning
3. The trend mechanic
4. The misconception / false choice being challenged
5. The Victoria-specific better possibility
6. Brand guardrails
7. Cultural / tone fit

## Pass criteria
The idea passes only if:
- the sound is doing real work in the joke/message
- the on-screen text sets up a belief or limitation the audio can rebut
- the message reinforces Victory positioning
- the wording sounds natural for Victoria
- the text is understandable in seconds
- it is specific enough to classify the niche
- it does not imply Victory recommends CAT work as the goal
- it does not imply people need multiple jobs to reach six figures

## Fail criteria
Reject if:
- the text is generic career advice that could sit under any audio
- the niche is pasted onto the trend without matching the sound's logic
- the message is technically clever but contradicts Victory positioning
- wording is too formal, consultant-like, or abstract
- the punchline / informational loop does not close

## Decision rule
Ask:
"If I muted this sound and replaced it with any random trending audio, would the text work exactly the same?"

If YES, the adaptation is probably too generic.

If NO because the sound itself completes or strengthens the meaning, the fit is stronger.
